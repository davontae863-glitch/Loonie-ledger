## Before posting

- "2025 → 2031" is our own arithmetic on CRA's rule for an individual (tax year = calendar year), assuming the 2025 return is filed on time. Records that relate to later years (e.g. an asset you keep claiming CCA on) are kept six years from the end of the *last* year they relate to — so keep "at least" in the copy.
- Objections/appeals (keep until resolved or six years, whichever is later) are in the pin description only, not the video, for length.
- Records kept outside Canada / electronic-records rules were left out — worth a future post ("Can I just keep photos of my receipts?") once checked against IC05-1.
- The tracker is described only as logging expenses by T2125 line — it doesn't store receipt images, so don't say it does.

## Sources checked (2026-11-13)

- CRA, "Where to keep your records, for how long and how to request the permission to destroy them early" (page date 2026-08-03) — keep records six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to (calendar year for individuals); late-filed return → six years from the date filed; objections/appeals → until the latest of resolution, appeal deadline or six years; keep at your place of business or residence in Canada unless CRA permits otherwise; destroying early needs written permission (Form T137 or in writing to your tax services office): https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html
- CRA, "Business records" (date modified 2026-08-31) — keep records a minimum of six years from the end of the last tax year to which they relate; supporting documents include sales invoices, receipts, bank deposit slips, bank statements, cancelled cheques; no receipt → record seller's name and address, amount, date and details: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html
