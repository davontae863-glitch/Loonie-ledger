## Before posting

- These are CRA's requirements for documents that **support a client's input tax credit claim**. They apply when you're a GST/HST registrant billing a business client; small suppliers who aren't registered don't put a GST/HST number on invoices (that's day 9). Keep "If you charge GST/HST" on screen.
- "Clients need it to claim" — CRA requires the details for the ITC claim; the video says "can hold up their claim" rather than "will be denied", on purpose.
- Tier 2 alternative: instead of the tax amount, the invoice can say the total includes GST/HST at the applicable rate. Mixed taxable/exempt invoices also need each item's status. Both left off the video for length; the pin description mentions the first.
- "Most freelancers forget" was dropped from the calendar hook — we have no data for it. The pin note says "Easy to miss" instead.

## Sources checked (2026-11-08)

- CRA, "Input tax credits" (date modified 2026-10-14) — information required on invoices/receipts to support an ITC claim, by total: under $100 (supplier's business or trading name, invoice date, total amount paid or payable); $100–$499.99 (plus the GST/HST charged or a statement that the total includes it, status of each supply if mixed, and the supplier's GST/HST registration number); $500 or more (plus the buyer's name or trading name, a brief description, and the terms of payment): https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html
- CRA, "Charge and collect the tax – Receipts and invoices" — you have to give customers who are GST/HST registrants specific information on invoices and receipts when you supply taxable goods and services: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-receipts-invoices.html
