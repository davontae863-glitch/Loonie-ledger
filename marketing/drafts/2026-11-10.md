## Before posting

- This is the **self-employed** rule (Form T2125, line 9945). Employees working from home use a different form (T2200 / T777) and rules — not covered; keep "self-employed" in the caption.
- The $2,000 / $2,500 and 150 / 1,500 sq ft figures are illustrative examples, not CRA numbers — keep the "Example" label on screen.
- "Can only bring your business income down to zero": CRA's wording is you deduct the lesser of the home expenses (plus carry-forward) and your net business income before them. Simplified on purpose.
- Left out for length: if the space is also used personally, CRA also prorates by business hours ÷ 24; claiming CCA on your home can trigger recapture and capital gains rules when you sell — worth a future post, not a casual "claim it all" tip.

## Sources checked (2026-11-10)

- CRA, "Business-use-of-home expenses" (Form T2125 guide, date modified 2026-08-31) — deductible if the workspace is your principal place of business, or used only to earn business income and on a regular and ongoing basis to meet clients, customers or patients; maintenance costs (heating, home insurance, electricity, cleaning materials) plus part of property taxes, mortgage interest and CCA; business part on a reasonable basis such as workspace area ÷ total home area (mixed use: business hours ÷ 24); cannot increase or create a business loss; unused amounts can be used in the next fiscal period; calculated in T2125 Part 7, claimed on line 9945: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/business-use-home-expenses.html
