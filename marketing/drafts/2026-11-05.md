## Before posting

- "December 31 year-end" is CRA's general rule — the page says "generally" and some eligible individuals can use another fiscal period. The script says "most", the pin says "generally"; keep it that way.
- Step 2 (odometer): CRA's wording is "start and end of the fiscal period". It's fine as "year" for a December 31 year-end, but don't add claims about logbook rules or deduction amounts (that's day 11's topic).
- Step 2 (GST): the four-quarter rule switches on at the end of the month after the quarter you go over, while going over in a single quarter means registration from that sale. The video keeps it short ("kicks in") on purpose; day 16 covers the detail. Don't say "you must register on January 1".
- Left out on purpose: December 15 instalment payments. The only CRA page found today lists the 2027 dates, not this December — don't add it unless you check it yourself.
- Use dummy numbers in the checker screen recording.

## Sources checked (2026-11-05)

- CRA, "Fiscal period" (date modified 2026-08-31) — "Self-employed individuals generally have to use a December 31 year-end"; eligible individuals may use an alternative method with a non-December 31 fiscal period: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/fiscal-period.html
- CRA, "When to register for and start charging the GST/HST" (date modified 2026-06-16) — $30,000 small-supplier threshold; single calendar quarter vs. last four (or fewer) consecutive calendar quarters: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html
- CRA, "Motor vehicle expenses" (date modified 2026-08-17) — "Record the odometer reading of each vehicle at the start and end of the fiscal period"; trip entries include date, destination, purpose, kilometres: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed-income/business-income-tax-reporting/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses.html
- CRA, "Business records" (date modified 2026-08-31) — required by law to keep records of all transactions; keep deposit slips, bank statements, cancelled cheques; with no receipt, record the seller's name and address, amount, date and details: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-records.html
