- **Description:** Crossed the $30,000 GST/HST threshold? When you have to start charging depends on how you crossed it. If your taxable sales (plus your associates') go over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter, CRA says you stop being a small supplier right then and have to charge GST/HST on the sale that took you over — and register within 29 days of your effective date of registration. If you go over $30,000 across four consecutive calendar quarters but not in any one quarter, you stop being a small supplier at the end of the month after the quarter you went over, and start charging from your effective date of registration (no later than your first sale after that). ## Before posting

- "Charge GST now" in the hook is short for CRA's wording "charge GST/HST on the supply that made you exceed $30,000". The body says it exactly — keep scene 4 and the caption line.
- The "end of the next month" rule is CRA's "end of the month following the quarter in which you exceed $30,000"; you start charging from your effective date of registration (no later than your first sale after that). Kept short in the video; the pin description has the full wording.
- The $30,000 counts taxable sales worldwide, including your associates', before expenses. Left out of the video for length (day 9 covered associates).
- Exceptions left out: taxi/ride-sharing drivers (must register regardless), public service bodies ($50,000), digital-economy/non-resident rules. "Not tax advice" covers it — don't drop it.
- The $2,000 / $10,000 / $38,000 figures are CRA's own worked example (the page uses 2020 dates) — labelled "CRA's example", not a customer story.
- Check posted.md for the 11-15 evening Reel row before numbering this one #11.
- The checker is described only as checking your quarters — don't say it tells you your registration date unless the owner confirms it does.
